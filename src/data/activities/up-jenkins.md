title: 'Up Jenkins'
type: 'game'
draft: false
excerpt: "Two teams across a table: one passes a coin hand to hand underneath, and the other team's captain has to find which hand is hiding it."
origin: "As played at the Moyers' in Pennsylvania (and, as Jenkins Up, in Iowa farming communities). Written up in Of Play and Playfulness (ECRS, 1990)."
objective: 'To identify the hand under which the coin is hidden.'
instructions: |
  **Formation:** Players sit at a long table, team A and team B facing each other. Four to seven players on each team works best.

  Team A passes the coin from hand to hand under the table. The coin can go in either direction but must be kept moving. The other team will eventually have to find the hand which holds the coin.

  The captain of team B for this round (players take turns being the captain) calls "Up Jenkins." All the players on team A must place their elbows on the table, arms upright and fists closed. The captain of team B then says "Down Jenkins." Immediately, team A must place their hands flat on the table.

  Team B's captain, in consultation with other members of the team, points *one at a time* to the hands which they think DO NOT HAVE THE COIN. These hands are turned over. (The team members offer their opinions, but the captain decides and is the only one who can point to turn over an opponent's hand.) The score for that round is the number of empty hands that have been turned over.

  Then team B passes the coin and team A's captain calls "Up Jenkins," "Down Jenkins," and turns up the empty hands.
leadingTips: 'Everyone is included: small children and adults all contribute suggestions, opinions, and hunches to their team.'
variations: |
  **Jenkins Up** (as played in Iowa farming communities): equipment, formation, and object are the same. Each team selects a captain, rotated each round. One team passes a quarter from hand to hand under the table. When the opposing captain says "Jenkins Up," the passers close their hands into fists and place their elbows on the table, arms upright. When the captain says "Jenkins Down," the passers bump their elbows on the table in unison three times, counting one-two-three, and on the fourth count all hands are slapped palm-down on the table.

  The watching team's captain, in consultation with teammates, can either eliminate hands that *don't* have the quarter (leaving the hand with the quarter until last), or "spot" the hand *with* the coin. If the captain is wrong, that game is lost and the original passers pass again. If the captain is correct, the team has won the round and the quarter changes sides. No score is kept, and a winning streak of three in a row is all that is allowed before the quarter changes sides.

title: 'Ti Li Cum'

excerpt: 'A gentle hand game for two in the family of Rock, Paper, Scissors, with signals for Water, Fire, and Land.'
origin: 'North American Native peoples, as given in Of Play and Playfulness (ECRS, 1990).'
objective: 'To make the signal which is dominant over that of the opponent. Water wins over Fire (quenches it); Fire wins over Land (destroys it); Land wins over Water (absorbs it).'
instructions: |
  **Formation:** Two players facing each other.

  Each player chooses one of the three forces and the hand signal which represents it:

  - **Water:** arm straight out, hand pointing down.
  - **Fire:** arm straight out, hand pointing up.
  - **Land:** arm straight out, hand flat.

  Players approach each other, extend an arm with fist closed and say, "Ti." Next draw arm back and say, "Li." Then extend arm out saying, "Cum" and simultaneously make the decided-upon signal. The dominant signal wins each time.
leadingTips: "Ti Li Cum's gentle quality comes from its symbolism of the unity of nature and from its subtle hand signals. Played by partners with individual decisions each round, it lets players devise strategies from an opponent's past choices. Hal Kantor warns against presenting it as a team game: the aggressiveness of team confrontation overpowers that gentle quality."

title: 'Doudlebska Polka'

draft: false
excerpt: 'A wheelchair version of the high-spirited Czech polka mixer: polka anywhere, march the circle humming, then chairs clap while pushers circle and choose a new chair.'
origin: "Eddie Moyer's wheelchair version, from the Special Populations chapter of Of Play and Playfulness (ECRS, 1990)."
instructions: |
  **Formation:** Individual chairs and partners anywhere on the floor.

  **First figure:** Couples dance anywhere around the floor, zigging and zagging around to polka music (16 polka steps, 32 counts).

  **Second figure:** Couples form a circle, marching around counter-clockwise humming la-la-la until the end of the phrase (16 steps, 32 counts).

  **Third figure:** Chairs are pushed to the center of the circle so that they can touch hands. Chairs clap hands twice and touch their neighbor on each side once, in time with the music. Pushers dance around the circle behind the chairs, counter-clockwise (16 steps, 32 counts).

  At the end of the phrase, choose a chair and repeat the dance from the beginning.
leadingTips: |
  From Eddie Moyer's notes on wheelchair dancing:

  - Pattern dances work best, with no emphasis on foot or hand movement. Partners stand side by side 15 inches apart to avoid traffic accidents, and keep that distance when dancing.
  - Every "step" takes 2 beats: one to grab the wheel and the other to push or pull (push, grab, push, hold... pull, grab, pull, hold is 2 steps forward and 2 steps back). Always keep in time.
  - Wheels need more than twice the space of dancers on foot, and figures like the do-si-do need room and about 24 beats. Promenading slows up the dance.
  - Do not use singing calls, which are set for "two good feet." Use straight calls.
  - Chairs with small wheels in front dance best, and good tires won't slip. Dancers who can push their chairs but not clap hands can clap the arms of their chairs, keeping their hands close to the wheels.
  - Ed's advice to leaders: know your material and your group, what they can and can't do. Treat each person with respect, and don't minimize the limitations.

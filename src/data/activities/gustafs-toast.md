title: "Gustaf's Toast"

excerpt: 'A stately Swedish square: couples advance and bow to "Gustaf," then dash under arches and swing home, with extra players stealing partners.'
origin: "Sweden (the book's sheet music marks the tune Swedish). Also known as Gustaf's Skoal. Written up in Of Play and Playfulness (ECRS, 1990)."
instructions: |
  **Formation:** Players form sets of eight, allowing 7 or 8 feet of space for each set. One or more free players may stand outside each set and attempt to steal partners.

  Players all sing. Head and foot couples (1 and 3) advance three steps toward each other, make a quick bob of the head (on the word "Gustaf"), walk backward 3 steps to place and bring feet together (measures 1-4). Side couples (2 and 4) do the same (measures 5-8). Repeat the entire sequence (measures 1-8 again).

  The chorus ("tra, la, la...") is sung in quicker time. Head couples advance and exchange partners. At the same time, side couples (2 and 4) join hands to make an arch. The 1st man and 3rd woman join hands and turn away from the center to go under the arch made by couple 4, while the 1st woman and 3rd man turn and go under the arch made by couple 2. They release hands and clap (on the first beat of measure 13), skip quickly back to their original place and join both hands with their original partner. Leaning away from each other, both couples swing vigorously around in place (measures 9-16).

  The same action is then repeated by side couples, who advance, change partners and go under arches made by couples 1 and 3 (measures 9-16).

  **Stealing:** A person without a partner may "steal" by standing in the home place of a dancing person who is in the process of going under an arch and coming back to swing. The "thief" steps in and swings with one of the returning dancers, and the other one must then step out and become a stealer in the next round.

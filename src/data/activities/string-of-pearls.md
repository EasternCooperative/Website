origin: 'Improv folk tradition'

instructions: |
  The leader asks for someone willing to repeat themselves a lot. That person
  stands in front of the group and says the opening line of a story, usually a
  single sentence. The leader then asks for someone to say the last line of the
  story, and emphasizes that the two lines do not need to connect or make sense
  yet. The first person repeats their opening line and the last person says
  theirs.

  The leader then asks for volunteers one by one to fill in the middle. As
  people join, they stand in a line facing the audience, shoulders aligned,
  with some space between them. A volunteer can slot in at any point in the
  line except before the first line or after the last line, which stay fixed.

  Every time someone new joins, the group tells the story again from the
  beginning, including the new line when it comes up. Play continues until the
  gaps are filled and the group is satisfied with the story.
leadingTips: |
  If the group is low on confidence, suggest the opening volunteer start with
  "Once upon a time."

  Encourage people to slot into particular spots if the story needs it. If
  people start jumping up and forming the line without being prompted, let that
  happen.

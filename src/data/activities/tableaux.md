origin: 'From the informal dramatics outline developed by Jim and Ruth Norris and Pat Emerson, a long-time resource for ECRS leaders. Written up in Of Play and Playfulness (ECRS, 1990).'
instructions: |
  A tableau is a dramatic picture which tells a story. The group creates the picture itself, which gives them a chance for real dramatic expression instead of being posed by the director in stiff, uncomfortable positions, as is done so often.

  Rehearse the story as if it were to be presented as a skit, with action and dialogue. When the story reaches its climax, the action is "frozen!" A picture should be held three minutes during rehearsal and one minute during performance. This trains the actors to hold the picture until the audience has time to take in the emotional meaning of the tableau.
variations: 'Combine tableaux with a reader, or with a singing chorus and orchestra as background.'

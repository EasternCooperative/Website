title: 'Swat'
type: 'game'
draft: false
excerpt: 'The player with the newspaper swat taps someone, sets the swat on the stool, and races back to their seat before getting swatted.'
origin: 'Of Play and Playfulness (ECRS, 1990)'
objective: "To replace the swat on the center stool and return to one's seat without being swatted."
instructions: |
  **Formation:** 12-20 players seated in chairs in a circle, with an extra chair or stool in the center.

  One player (e.g. Marie) has the swat, walks around the inside of the circle and taps one of the other players (e.g. Louis) below the knee. She then must place the swat on the center stool and return to her own seat. In the meantime, Louis gets out of his chair, picks up the swat from the center stool and attempts to swat Marie before she sits down.

  If Marie succeeds in eluding the swat, she remains in her chair and Louis begins the game again.

  If Louis succeeds in swatting Marie before she gains the safety of her chair, he in turn places the swat on the center stool, and Marie has to retrieve it and try to swat Louis again. This turn-around continues until one of the two players gets safely back into his or her own chair.

  If the swat falls to the floor, the player whose responsibility it was to place it on the center stool must pick it up and place the swat on the stool; the game continues from that point. (This rule should be explained only after the swat is dropped.)
leadingTips: 'Ed Moyer taught Swat at his last games workshop, in 1986, and the Altoona Community Support Program kept using it more than any other: it quickly creates interest and interaction and gives exercise, and players have to move quickly, make decisions, and take on changing roles. They play it fast with young adults and at a slower pace with older groups.'
safetyNotesOther: 'Chairs can tip over as players rush back to them. Tables can be arranged behind the chairs to support them, or players can take turns (two at a time) standing behind the empty chairs to keep them from toppling over. These guards sometimes get absorbed in the action of the game and need to be reminded to keep moving to the empty chair.'
formation: 'Circle of chairs around a stool'
groupSizeMin: 12
groupSizeMax: 20
materialsNeeded: 'A chair for each player, an extra stool or chair, and a "swat" made of rolled up newspaper secured with tape.'

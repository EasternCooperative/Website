title: 'Skeleton Story'

excerpt: 'Every small group gets the same dramatic situation, such as finding a wallet or a fire breaking out, and makes it into its own skit.'
origin: 'From the informal dramatics outline developed by Jim and Ruth Norris and Pat Emerson, a long-time resource for ECRS leaders. Written up in Of Play and Playfulness (ECRS, 1990).'
instructions: |
  Break the group up into small units (no more than ten people). The same dramatic situation is given to each unit. For example, you are a group of people somewhere engaged in some activity and:

  - There is a holdup. What does the group do about it?
  - The group finds a wallet.
  - A fire breaks out.
  - Each group creates a situation that must end with the words, "Oh, my goodness, it's 12 o'clock!"

  Each unit creates its own story, characterizations, and production, including properties, costumes, and sets. Often people portray inanimate objects themselves: a turnstile, a water cooler, a slot machine, typewriters, showers, automobiles. Properties consist of whatever is at hand: a ping pong paddle for a tongue depressor, a coat hanger for the horns of a bull, a large sieve as a catcher's mask, a lamp shade for a hat. At a given time the whole group comes back together and acts as audience for each presentation.
leadingTips: |
  Although this kind of dramatics is improvised and spontaneous, it should have form in order to be enjoyed by both the actors and the audience.

  - When looking for a plot, explore more than one story. Let the group choose. Always tell them they can talk; they will always ask.
  - As soon as your actors get a story and the characters needed to portray it, get them into rehearsal, or they will talk themselves to death.
  - A skit is created on its feet. You may have to fix certain cues and entrances, but otherwise ad lib it.
  - Be specific. When working out the story, ask: Who? Where? What? When? Stick to your story; your actors love to ramble.
  - Let the actors cast themselves as much as possible.
  - Make the beginnings and ends of scenes clean and clear. Have one of your actors call "curtain" good and loud at the beginning and end of each scene.

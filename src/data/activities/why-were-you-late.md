excerpt: 'An improv guessing game with an audience where a guesser works out why they were late to their own job, how they got there, and where they work, from clues their coworkers mime.'
origin: 'Popularized by Whose Line Is It Anyway?'
relatedLeaderIds: ['isaac-lebwohl-steiner']
instructions: |
  One person leaves the room, somewhere out of earshot. The leader coordinates
  the group in choosing three things:

  - the job, for example a candy factory, horse stables, or a military base
  - why the guesser was late, for example their dog ran away, they were attacked
    by flamingos, or they ate a bee
  - how they got to work, for example they stole a unicycle from a bear,
    hang-glided, or got a ride from their roommate

  One volunteer is chosen as the boss. Others who want to play workers form a
  line off to the side, or get up when an opportunity arises.

  The guesser returns and the boss berates them for being late. The guesser
  does not know why they were late, but while the boss talks, two of the workers
  silently act out a clue to the question "Why were you late?" The guesser and
  boss talk it through, the guesser pitching guesses. The boss rejects every
  wrong guess incredulously, then, when the guesser lands it, makes a show of
  how much sense it makes. The boss then asks how the guesser got to work, and
  the workers act that out too.

  Now and then the boss turns around to check on the workers. If the boss
  catches them not working in a way that fits the workplace, the boss can
  threaten to fire them or actually fire them. A fired worker leaves the stage
  and the next volunteer takes their place.
leadingTips: |
  If the guesser is having trouble, have someone "call" the boss on the phone
  and hold an in-scene conversation that gives a clue.
variations: |
  Play without firing, and with a fixed number of players.

excerpt: 'Small groups choose an interesting tree, hold an imaginary conversation with it, and then lead everyone to it to tell its story.'
origin: 'Contributed by Martha Moss to Of Play and Playfulness (ECRS, 1990).'
instructions: |
  **Formation:** Outdoors, in a location with several large trees; informal groups of 2-6 players.

  The leader provides note paper and pencil if desired. Each group is directed to find a tree that they agree is interesting (in shape, size, location, etc.), to hold an imaginary conversation with that tree, and to be prepared to report back to the larger group some of the "information" they got from their tree.

  The leader suggests, orally or with a written list, some questions to ask the tree, such as: What is your name? How old are you? What was it like here when you were a seedling? Can you tell us some interesting event that has happened here in your lifetime? How long do you expect to be here? What do you think will happen to you when you leave? The leader instructs the group to return to a central meeting place in about 15 minutes.

  When the larger group gathers again, small groups take turns guiding the others to their tree and telling the tree's story. Allow about 5-8 minutes for traveling to each tree and hearing its story.
leadingTips: |
  If the available area is large, designate boundaries, so that a group will not use the time wandering about to select the perfect tree.

  As described here it is a group activity, but it can also be a play experience for one or two.

instructions: |
  In this version of the popular game, there is no pressure to participate. Members may participate when they are ready. The focus is on the fun in acting and not on competition for speed or the use of shortcut signals. All ages can enjoy it.

  Players are seated on chairs, benches, or couches in a semicircle. A player thinks of a slogan, a saying, a proverb, the title of a song, a book, a poem, the first line of a poem, a word, etc. Whatever it is, the attempt should be made to select something with acting possibilities.

  When a player has something in mind, they offer it to anyone who wants to act it out. The information is whispered to the volunteer, who proceeds to act it out for the rest of the group. The clear space at the far side of the semicircle can be a natural stage.

  As the volunteer acts, players try to guess what is being acted. The actor may not talk or make a sound, but may nod to indicate if a suggestion is hot or cold.

  A few simple signs may be helpful to indicate categories:

  - The number of fingers held up indicates the number of words.
  - Fingers extended on one wrist indicate syllables. The number of fingers indicates which syllable is being acted.
  - Extending the arms in a broad circular motion means the player is acting out the entire idea of the saying, not the individual words.
  - Motioning with both hands behind one's back indicates that the player is acting out background material.

  When the solution has been found, the volunteer resumes their place in the circle. Another player volunteers a slogan and someone else offers to act it out.
leadingTips: 'Charades can be fun if done as theater rather than as a pun or as mental gymnastics: each scene should have action, story, and characterization (Norris and Emerson). Things to dramatize include words (parade, rainbow, pumpkin, breakfast, earthquake, nightmare), advertising slogans, quotations ("Don''t shoot until you see the whites of their eyes"), proverbs ("Haste makes waste"), plays, song titles ("Easter Parade"), movies, and books.'
